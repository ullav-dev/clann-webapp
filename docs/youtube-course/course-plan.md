# Clann YouTube Training Course — Master Plan

## Series: "Clann — Your Family, Your Story"

**Format:** 13 episodes · 4–5 minutes each · one clear outcome per video
**Audience:** B2C — people curious about their family history; non-technical
**Tone:** Warm, encouraging, occasionally surprising. Think "knowledgeable friend", not "help desk".

## Per-Episode Structure Template

Every episode follows this arc:

```
0:00–0:20   HOOK
            One punchy question or surprising fact. No intro music yet.
            Get them leaning in before they know what hit them.

0:20–0:35   WHAT YOU'LL LEARN
            One sentence. Sets expectation, reduces drop-off.

0:35–3:30   CORE DEMO
            Screen recording. Narrated step by step.
            2–3 beats with a verbal signpost between each.
            Show real, relatable data — not placeholder text.

3:30–4:15   TIPS & GOTCHAS
            1–2 "insider" tips. This is the moment viewers feel
            they're getting something they wouldn't find in the docs.

4:15–4:45   RECAP & CTA
            One breath summary of what they just did.
            Curiosity tease for the next episode.
            "Like if this helped. Subscribe for the next one."
```

## Production Notes — Mac Stack

### Recording
- **QuickTime** for screen recording — record the browser window only, not full screen
- Record at 1080p minimum (Retina captures 2x — scale down in iMovie)
- **Cursor Pro** (Mac app) — highlights clicks with a ripple; essential for tutorial content
- **Screenflow** (paid, ~$149) — step up from QuickTime; records system audio + mic simultaneously,
  has built-in callout and zoom tools worth having once you're past episode 3

### Audio
- Audio quality matters more than video quality on YouTube
- USB or lapel mic over MacBook built-in every time
- **Logic Pro:** Channel EQ + Noise Gate on voice track cleans up room noise effectively
- Record 10 seconds of room tone before each session — fills cuts seamlessly
- Soft room (books, curtains) beats treated studio for most home setups

### Editing — iMovie
- **Picture-in-picture** face-cam in corner for hook and CTA sections — builds trust fast
- **Ken Burns** zoom-ins to draw attention to specific UI elements
- Keep cuts tight — slow mouse movements are the #1 retention killer
- Text overlays for "Pro Tip" callouts — keeps visuals dynamic between screen segments

### YouTube Strategy
- **Chapters are essential** — timestamps in description matching script beats; YouTube renders
  them as a chapter bar and they appear in Google search snippets
- **First 30 seconds determines retention** — the algorithm weights average view percentage;
  a strong hook that avoids early drop-off outweighs a polished ending
- **Thumbnail formula:** large text (3–4 words max) + expressive face OR clear UI screenshot
  + consistent brand colour across the series
- **Playlists:** create "Clann Training Course" playlist immediately; YouTube auto-recommends
  the next video in the series
- **Upload cadence:** weekly ideal for a new channel; record 2 episodes before publishing Ep 1
  so you always have a buffer
- **Description structure:** first 2 lines = ad copy (they show in search), then timestamps,
  then Clann link, then boilerplate subscribe CTA

### Tone Principles
- Open with a relatable human moment, not a product feature
- Use real (or lovingly fictional) family data in demos — never "Test User" or "John Doe"
- Short "Did you know?" historical asides for the census/research episodes
- End every episode with curiosity bait toward the next:
  *"Next time, we're going to find a real person in the 1926 census —
  and you might be surprised what turns up."*
